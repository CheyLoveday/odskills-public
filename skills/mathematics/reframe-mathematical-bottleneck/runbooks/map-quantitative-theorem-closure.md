# Map a stalled quantitative theorem

Use when several guarded results or representations could pay parts of the
same quantitative goal. Start with the exact consumer, the original counted
objects, and the selected evidence at fixed revisions. Use the
[triage runbook](representation-bottleneck-triage.md) for a single chart
comparison or the [incidence runbook](incidence-budget-and-adverse-controls.md)
for a single catalogue-to-count step.

## Map the claim and its open populations

1. **Freeze the consumer.** State its quantifiers, target, counting unit,
   parameter range, constants and exceptions. Use Lamport-Plus if a hierarchical
   proof outline helps expose the dependencies. Give each retained claim a
   source declaration or location and revision. Record how its representation
   relates to the original objects and which implication directions are proved.
2. **Budget backward.** Prove the cover of the original population and keep
   a row for each required regime, including complements and boundaries.
   Mark required premises and populations as AND. Mark alternative sufficient
   routes to the same conclusion as OR. For each route derive the implication
   from the proposed local bound to the consumer result. Keep the candidate
   bound open until proved. Use the [theorem-budget card](../references/reverse-engineer-theorem-budgets.md)
   for signed exponent slack, after fibre, summation and uniformity costs.
3. **Propagate forward on the same source.** Check what proved or imported
   statements actually supply, at their exact versions. Identify the receiving
   claim, substitution and remaining guards. Where a proposed join needs one
   witness, exhibit the common witness or a proved compatibility map. Where
   separate witnesses suffice, state that weaker requirement. Align the scale,
   quantifiers and simultaneous guards. An identical projected label gives no
   witness equality. If a premise fails, keep the first precise bridge open.

## Compare, check and update

4. **Compare a representation only where it changes an open row.** Match the
   original counted unit. Charge clearing, height, divisibility, multiplicity,
   fibres and exceptions in the consumer's inequality. Reuse a qualified branch
   even if another fails. Never treat two marginal bounds as independent joint
   savings without a theorem supporting that step.
5. **Audit the load-bearing edges.** Select the mathematical claim, computational
   evidence, or Lean correspondence audit for the evidence actually in hand.
   Include a plausible wrong witness, omitted exceptional row or unsupported
   scope upgrade. Keep an ordinary proof, a conditional implication, a finite
   check, independent review and Lean correspondence separately identifiable.
6. **Recompute the cover.** Follow each qualified or conditional result into its
   named consumers and update only the rows it reaches. If an audit fails,
   restore the affected obligation and recheck its downstream dependencies.
   Stop at a justified complete cover or the first named open leaves with
   their exact obligations. A conditional row stays conditional in the summary.

Use a single overlap rule when multiple routes pay the same sources. For an
upper-bound exponent, report target minus the complete derived exponent and
say whether zero slack is admissible. Include constants and other losses where
exponent slack alone cannot settle the target.
