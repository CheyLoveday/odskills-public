# Checking a load-bearing implication

Use the actual definitions and source revision. Preserve domains, index/rank,
sign, orientation, normalization and witness identity through each map.

- `for every x there exists y` does not give one y working for every x. When
  reusing a fixed-object or fixed-time bound under selection, stopping or a
  changing sequence, identify what was held fixed and what the choice can
  depend on. Establish the required uniform, joint or conditional control;
  pointwise validity alone supplies none of those. For instance, convergence
  of each matrix's powers need not control mixed products. A valid common
  contraction bound can still justify the revised argument. Retain such
  independent branches instead of discarding every result with the bad step.
- An implication or necessary sieve filter does not establish its converse.
  An exclusion needs complete forward coverage; existence needs a construction
  satisfying all original conditions. A projection can lose eligibility data.
- Check zero, units, equality endpoints, exceptional/ramified primes, exact
  versus lower-bound valuations and division by a potentially nonunit/zero.
  Natural truncated subtraction and integer subtraction need a domain bridge.
- A smaller coordinate is not a descent unless the new object remains eligible
  and a well-founded measure strictly decreases.
- Independent true facts cannot be joined through different middle witnesses.
  Branches must cover the stated domain; mutually incompatible branches cannot
  be conjoined to construct a witness.
- A changed constant term, recurrence convention or coefficient basis may alter
  a detector even if its familiar name is unchanged. Check executable code,
  mathematics and explanatory comments as distinct representations.

For each suspect step, seek a small matched example or independent derivation.
Preserve the original defect and the limits of a repair: strengthening a premise
can establish a revised result without proving the original. Do not assume a
missing proof is false, or that a genuine local defect invalidates independent
parts of the argument.
