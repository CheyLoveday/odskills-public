# Consuming audit evidence

Load when several specialists, sources or revisions support one audit.

Bind each input to its target, source snapshot, domain, assumptions, witness or
map, evidence type, declared scope and actual outcome. Prefer the project's
existing record; no new schema or registry is required. A specialist's verdict
is evidence to inspect, not an extra premise establishing the theorem.

Check before combining:

1. Does the result address this target/revision and the exact required direction?
2. Are witnesses, fields, populations, units, bounds and normalizations compatible?
3. Was the needed action performed, merely planned, supplied historically, or
   unavailable? Does current evidence match its source and checker identities?
4. Does a failed or missing prerequisite block this conclusion? Keep independently
   supported results instead of marking every sibling failed or proved.
5. Was the review independent of the central work and of peer verdicts? A
   coordinator reading others' reports performs synthesis, not another independent
   review. Use a non-independent label when the authorship condition is unmet.

After a source or target revision, reassess dependent conclusions. Preserve the
old result and identities; do not silently relabel it as a check of the revision.
Bounded computation cannot replace a missing general lemma. A compiled weaker
statement cannot establish an unbridged stronger claim. Recommendations cannot
waive the consuming project's acceptance or execution rules.
